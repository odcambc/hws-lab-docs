# Sterilization, decontamination, and waste


## Filtration

### Pore size

| Nominal pore size | Use                                                 |
| ----------------- | --------------------------------------------------- |
| 0.22 µm           | Sterilizing filtration                              |
| 0.45 µm           | Clarifying filtration; not treated as sterilization |

Suitability also depends on the organism, solution, filter construction,
process volume, and validated sterility requirement.

### Membrane selection

| Membrane              | Typical use                    |
| --------------------- | ------------------------------ |
| PES                   | Aqueous solutions              |
| Hydrophilic PVDF      | Protein-containing solutions   |
| Hydrophobic PTFE      | Corrosive or organic solutions |
| Nylon                 | Aqueous solutions              |
| Cellulose acetate     | Aqueous solutions              |
| Regenerated cellulose | Aqueous solutions and solvents |

!!! warning "Check compatibility"
    Verify chemical compatibility, protein binding, extractables, temperature,
    pressure, and manufacturer guidance for the exact membrane and housing.

## Autoclaving

**121 °C at 15 psi for approximately 30 minutes** as a
general reference, with longer processing potentially required for a full
chamber.

| Load                 | Exhaust setting |
| -------------------- | --------------- |
| Liquids              | Slow exhaust    |
| Solids and dry goods | Fast exhaust    |

!!! important "Indicators"
    Autoclave tape indicates exposure to a threshold condition; it does not
    prove that the load remained at sterilizing conditions for the required
    time. Use the monitoring and validation required by the approved SOP.

### Materials listed as unsuitable

- Polystyrene
- Polyethylene
- Polyurethane

Material suitability depends on the exact product. Confirm its manufacturer
rating before autoclaving.

## Biological waste

The following captures the legacy workflow and requires EHS verification:

1. Place waste in the locally approved double-bag configuration.
2. Close the bag using the approved method and indicator tape.
3. Process it using the designated waste cycle.
4. After successful processing, apply the approved green **Safe to trash**
   label.
5. Leave the processed bag in the designated pickup bin near the autoclave.

!!! warning "Bag color and waste cycle unresolved"
    The legacy notes explicitly say not to use red biohazard bags, but do not
    identify the approved replacement bags or autoclave cycle. Confirm both
    with EHS before this workflow is used.

## Serological pipettes

The legacy workflow is:

1. Collect pipettes in an autoclavable rigid container with an approved liner.
2. Decontaminate using the validated autoclave waste cycle.
3. Apply the approved green **Safe to trash** label.
4. Move the processed container to the designated pickup location.

Confirm whether a cardboard collection box is currently permitted and whether
pipettes are classified as sharps under local policy.

## Sharps and chemical waste

No usable instructions were present in the legacy document.

!!! danger
    Do not place sharps or chemical waste into the biological-waste workflow.
    Use the current institutional collection containers and EHS procedures.
